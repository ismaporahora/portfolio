Scaling type without breaking hierarchy

Role
Product Designer — proposal, interaction design, frontend implementation
Timeline
August–September 2026 · contrast toggle 6 Aug, text size 4 Sep
Tools
Claude Code · CSS custom properties · Tailwind · Vitest
TL;DR — In a meeting with two people from the education world, both over 60, I had to zoom the screen for them several times: they couldn't read it and couldn't zoom on their own. I proposed an accessibility layer and pushed it through: one control on every screen, with high contrast and three text sizes, where small text grows more than large text.

The meeting

I met with two people from the education world, both over 60. During the meeting we had to zoom in several times: they couldn't read the screen, and they couldn't zoom it themselves.

The browser can already zoom, but they didn't know how. So the control had to be inside the product, visible on every screen, one tap away.

The problem with zooming everything

The interface is sized in literal pixels, down to the half pixel (case 05). What was hard to read was the small text: hints, dates, labels at 11 or 12 pixels. Taking an 11-pixel label to 14 with a uniform scale means +27%, which turns a 26-pixel title into 33 pixels and breaks the layouts.

Decision 1 · One control, always on screen

The first version was a Light / Dark / High contrast selector inside the user menu, where nobody from that meeting would have looked for it. Two days later it was a single button fixed to a corner of every screen, and dark mode was dropped.

In September the button opened a panel with high contrast and text size. Text size went from a segmented control to a cycling A+ button to a − A + stepper, which moves one step at a time; the A in the middle grows with the level. The choice is saved in a cookie and applied on the server, so pages load at the right size without a flash.

Decision 2 · Small text grows more

Each size has its own rule. On Grande, text grows 3 pixels at the small end, a tenth of a pixel less for each pixel above 12, never less than 2. Muy grande starts at +5, never less than 3. A 10.5-pixel hint grows 29%; a 26-pixel title, 8%. The hierarchy compresses but never inverts.

Decision 3 · High contrast means black

In high contrast every text is black: the four greys of the ink scale, and the semantic colors — violet, green, amber, red — when used as text. Backgrounds stay and borders get darker. Without the greys, the only hierarchy left is size and weight.

Decision 4 · The rule is enforced in the code

There is a type rule for each pixel size the app uses, 31 per level, and a test fails if someone adds a size without its rule. Contrast overrides the color tokens, so the two components with hardcoded colors were moved into the tokens. Same idea as the design system in case 05.

Status

Live for every user since September 2026 in the teachers' app and the admin console, and in the Dirección view for principals, which is still behind a flag.

CaveatThe panel doesn't send events, so I don't know how many people use it or which settings they pick.
